1. What you and Sir Peter asked for

Who attends the event, and who the client will meet

This is Sir Peter's named-staff decision from 29 October, built without individual staff logins.

PageAddressWhat to look for
My Gigs, a single gig/professional/gigs/{gig} A "Who attends" question with three answers: attend personally, send a staff member, or decide nearer the time. Naming a crew member as the on-site contact, and naming a supervisor. A confirm button for a gig where the answer was left open.
Team & Staffing/professional/team Each crew member now has a photo, a rank, and a reference number of their own. A supervisor can be marked as such.
Client's Bookings
(client side)		/client/bookings The client sees the name, photo and phone number of the person who will actually turn up, once the professional has named them.
Three rules inside this that are worth checking deliberately.
  • A professional who promised in the agreement to attend personally cannot then send a staff member. They have to ask for that term to change first.
  • A staff member's photo is not on the public internet. Opening the image address without being signed in shows nothing. Only the professional who owns it, an administrator, and the one client whose booking that person is attending can see it.
  • The client loses access to a staff member's contact details 30 days after the job finishes, unless a dispute is open. Sir Peter's own proposal.

Reminders when nobody has said who is coming

Sir Peter asked for this to be automatic whenever "Decide Later" is chosen and no contact has been assigned. There are three reminders, the last of them 24 hours before the service begins. Each one is recorded before it is sent, so the same reminder is never sent twice. A missed deadline is recorded.

Still your decision: the reminders go to the professional only. Whether the client should also be told that the deadline was missed has not been decided. The miss is recorded either way, so it can be turned on later.

Agreements, from the professional's side

This was the structural gap. Before this work, a professional could not open an agreement at all. Every agreement page belonged to the client.

PageAddressWhat to look for
My Agreements/professional/agreements A new section in the left menu. The list of agreements this professional is party to.
One agreement/professional/agreements/{id} What changed since they last approved, then approve, decline, request changes, or sign. A copy of the draft terms to download, which they had no way to keep before.
This settles an open question.

The deposit step used to be unblocked by a stand-in signature that the client's own signing action wrote on the professional's behalf. That is gone. No deposit can now be taken on an agreement that one side has not signed. So the answer to "may a client fund before the professional signs" is no.

Who keeps hiring me

PageAddressWhat to look for
My Clients/professional/clients A new page. The clients who have booked this professional, grouped by client rather than by event. Money is shown after commission, to match the Earnings page rather than contradict it.

2. What we found while building it

None of this was reported. It was found by opening the pages and checking what they were drawing.

PageAddressWhat was wrong
Whole portal/professional/* A signed-in client could open every one of the 80 professional pages. Nothing leaked, because each page only shows rows where the reader is the supplier, but that was luck rather than a boundary. Now refused.
Leads/professional/leads The conversion gauge showed 82% High to professionals who had never had a single lead. 82 was a number somebody typed as a fallback. Also: two messages nobody wrote, timed 11:30 and 11:32, under the real name and initials of that professional's most recent lead. And the budget figure printed its own programming code on screen.
Calendar/professional/calendar A travel time of 35 minutes, the same for a venue down the road and one three hours away, with a "Leave By" time worked out from it. Someone believing that arrives late. Also availability switches that were always on and were connected to nothing, so a professional could believe they had marked themselves free.
Priority Actions/professional/priority-actions The panels headed "Example:" were filled from the reader's own newest booking, so a real client's name appeared under an invented quote about a child's birthday, and a real venue address beside a made-up guest count. With no booking the page invented one. Worst of it, a clock counted down from 4 hours 32 minutes, once a second, on an offer that does not exist.
Gig Hub/professional/gig-hub A full worked example of somebody else's job: a drawn map, "1 hr 23 min (84.6 mi)", a team chat, a food inventory, and a 120 guest $4,000 booking belonging to nobody.
Bidding Board/professional/bidding-board A card that said Open and Closed at the same time. The Save button looked like it worked and saved nothing, so it was connected to the saving that already existed. A Share button and a market insights button did nothing and were removed.
Bid Intelligence/professional/bid-intelligence A recommendation the page had no figures to support.
Profile, Verification tab/professional/profile It asked professionals to send in documents in exchange for a badge that "appears on your public profile and in search results". Badges were switched off across the site on 23 September, so the badge does not appear anywhere. The same sentence also named a competitor by name.
Messages/professional/messages The message list filled up with finished jobs and never emptied. Threads for completed work now tidy themselves away, and a thread somebody deliberately brought back stays back.
Agreements/professional/agreements Every agreement recorded "the professional will attend personally" even when nobody had chosen it, because that was the stored default and no agreement is created with the question answered. That wording then blocked the professional from sending a staff member, and the contract showed it to the client as an agreed term.
My Gigs/professional/gigs/{gig} Reporting a problem existed as a page and a menu item, but there was no way into it from the job itself. You had to find your own booking in a dropdown. There are now links from the booking, the event and the gig.
Six bannersvarious Promised outcomes: "Win More", "close more deals", "convert more leads", "profitable and stress-free". Removed, against Sir Peter's rule that the platform does not promise results.
